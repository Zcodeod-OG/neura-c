What is there, where, and how near?

Each trial is one scene a head-height depth camera saw: one object somewhere in front of you. You get it in one of two codes, then say what it is, which direction, and whether it is nearer than 3 metres. There is no practice and no feedback until the end, because the question is whether the code makes sense the first time.

The two codes

  • Sound code. A short beep comes from the object's direction (far left to far right in your headphones). One slow beep means far; three quick beeps mean near, like a parking sensor. Then a voice says what the object is.
  • Dot code. An 8 × 8 grid of dots shows the object's outline, blurred to what skin can resolve. Where it sits in the grid is its direction; bigger and brighter means nearer.

20 scenes per code, about 6 minutes in all. The order of the codes is random. Check your headphones first.
